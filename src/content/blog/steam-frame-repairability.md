Is Valve's new headset easy to fix? **Steam Frame repairability** just got its first hard test, and iFixit's teardown brings mostly good news with two real catches: a glued battery and a fan buried under the optics.

The timing helps buyers. Valve launched the Steam Frame on September 14, 2026, at $1,059 for the 256GB kit and $1,299 for the 1TB kit. When a headset costs that much, how long you can keep it running matters as much as the specs.

Here's what iFixit found, which parts you can replace yourself, and whether repairability should change your buying decision.

## Steam Frame Repairability at a Glance

iFixit did not assign a repairability score, but the verdict is clear from the details. It praised Valve's thoughtful design in most places, and Valve stumbled in a few.

| 🔧 Part | 📊 Verdict | 📝 What iFixit found |
|---|---|---|
| 👃 Nose bridge | ✅ Great | Modular and replaceable on its own, a step beyond what iFixit has seen on other headsets |
| 🔍 Lens assemblies | ✅ Great | Held by magnets, easy to remove and reinstall |
| 🔩 Fasteners | ✅ Good | Mostly screws and snaps that need only mild pressure |
| 🔋 Battery | ❌ Poor | Glued to the housing, needed isopropyl alcohol and careful prying |
| 🌀 Fan | ⚠️ Frustrating | Removal means disturbing the optical assemblies and dense internals |
| 🎮 Controller joystick | ❌ Poor | Full-size TMR stick is soldered in |

## What Valve Got Right

**The modular nose bridge and magnetic lenses stand out.** The nose bridge replaces on its own, so a cracked or worn piece doesn't mean tearing down the whole front of the headset. The lenses attach with magnets, which makes cleaning, swapping, and upgrades much easier.

Most of the body comes apart with screws and snaps. That is a big deal for a device you wear on your face and sweat into. Face gaskets, cushions, and light blockers are the parts that wear out first, and they are the easiest to reach.

## Where the Steam Frame Falls Short

### The battery is glued in

The battery is a curved two-cell lithium-polymer pack rated at 20.9 Wh by iFixit (Valve lists 21.6 Wh). It is glued to the inside of the housing with no easy-release mechanism. iFixit needed isopropyl alcohol and careful prying to get it out, per [vr.org's teardown summary](https://vr.org/articles/steam-frame-ifixit-teardown-battery-glued-fan-2026).

[Editorial note: batteries are the part of any headset that wears out first. A glued pack is the biggest long-term worry here.]

### The fan is the "clearest missed opportunity"

Swapping a fan should be a five-minute job. On the Steam Frame, getting to it means working past the optical assemblies and through a dense internal stack. iFixit called it the design's clearest missed opportunity. Headset fans tend to collect dust and sweat, so this one will matter.

### The controller joystick is soldered

The Steam Frame controller uses a full-size TMR joystick, which is a good sign for drift resistance. But it is soldered to the board, so replacing it takes soldering skills, not a screwdriver.

## Can You Buy Replacement Parts?

Yes, with limits. iFixit says it will sell Steam Frame spares and publish repair guides, the same approach it took with the Steam Deck. The first wave, through the Valve Repair Hub, covers face gaskets, head cushions, and light blockers.

⚠️ **Batteries are not in the first wave.** iFixit said it won't sell Steam Frame batteries when the parts store goes live. Watch for that to change, because the battery is the part most owners will eventually need.

## How to Make Your Steam Frame Last Longer

Repairability is a backup plan. Good habits keep you from needing it. [Editorial note: these are general headset-care tips, not Valve guidance, so check the official manual for anything specific.]

- ✅ **Wipe the face gasket and cushions after each session.** Sweat is what wears them out, and they are the parts you can replace cheaply.
- ✅ **Clean the lenses with a dry microfiber cloth.** Skip sprays and paper towels, which can scratch coatings.
- ✅ **Keep the vents clear.** A dusty fan runs harder, and the fan is one of the hardest parts to reach.
- ✅ **Avoid heat and direct sun.** Lithium batteries age faster when they sit hot.
- ❌ **Don't leave it plugged in at 100% for weeks.** If you take a long break, store it partly charged.

All of it is free, and it postpones the day you have to face that glued battery.

## FAQ

### Is the Steam Frame easy to repair?

Mostly yes. iFixit found a modular nose bridge, magnetic lens assemblies, and screws and snaps that come apart with mild pressure. The weak spots are a glued battery, a fan that sits under the optics, and a soldered controller joystick. iFixit did not give it a repairability score.

### Can you replace the Steam Frame battery?

Technically yes, but it is difficult. The battery is glued to the housing, and iFixit needed isopropyl alcohol and careful prying to remove it. iFixit also said it won't sell Steam Frame batteries when its parts store launches, so you'll need to source one another way.

### How much does the Steam Frame cost?

The 256GB kit costs $1,059 and the 1TB kit costs $1,299. Each kit includes the Steam Frame controllers, a Wi-Fi 6E adapter for PC streaming, and a copy of Half-Life: Alyx. Valve released it on September 14, 2026, and ships units as they become available.

### Will iFixit sell Steam Frame parts?

Yes. iFixit will sell replacement parts and publish repair guides through the Valve Repair Hub. The first parts are face gaskets, head cushions, and light blockers. Batteries are not included at launch, and iFixit did not give a date for adding them.

## The Bottom Line

Valve built a headset that opens up like a device meant to last, then glued in the one part that will age first. It is a strong start and a fixable flaw. Keep an eye on the Valve Repair Hub, and if you're buying at $1,059, ask whether you can live with a hard battery swap in a few years.
