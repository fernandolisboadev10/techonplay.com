Most people type “make this look professional” into ChatGPT and get the same flat, overlit render everyone else gets. **ChatGPT image prompts** stop being generic the moment you tell the model exactly how to light, frame, and shoot the scene, not just what to put in it.

That gap between “AI-looking” and “actually usable” content matters more now than ever. Brands, creators, and marketers are shipping dozens of visuals a week, and the ones that stand out are the ones built with real photographic language instead of vague adjectives.

This guide breaks down 100 visual command codes across 10 categories, from lighting to product photography, so you can direct ChatGPT’s image generator like a photographer instead of hoping for the best.

### Why Generic Prompts Produce Generic Images

ChatGPT’s image model responds to specificity. When you write “a person in an office,” it fills in every gap with the most statistically average choice: flat light, centered framing, neutral expression. Nothing wrong, nothing memorable either. ### Lighting Commands That Set the Mood

Lighting decides whether an image reads as a stock photo or an editorial shot. These ten commands control where the light comes from and how hard it hits the subject.

-   💡 **spotlight** — carves the subject out of darkness, theater-style
-   🌅 **goldenhour** — warm, late-day sun with stretched shadows
-   🌙 **silhouette** — clean dark shape against a bright backdrop
-   🔦 **rimlight** — a thin bright outline lifts the subject off the background
-   🎨 **chiaroscuro** — painterly extremes of light and shadow

### Surreal Commands For Scroll-Stopping Concepts

When a normal photo won’t cut it, these commands push the scene into impossible territory while keeping it visually coherent, ideal for thumbnails and social hooks.

-   🌀 **portal** — a doorway linking two different worlds in one frame
-   🪶 **gravityoff** — everything floats, as if gravity quit its job
-   🔁 **infinite** — one element repeats into a hypnotic distance
-   🏙️ **giantobject** — an everyday item becomes a monument-sized landmark

### Framing Commands That Control Focus

Framing decides what the eye lands on first. These ten commands are the difference between a cluttered image and one with clear visual hierarchy.

-   🖼️ **negativeSpace** — generous empty space, great for adding text overlays
-   🔍 **closeup** — lets face, texture, or product detail carry the frame
-   ⚖️ **symmetry** — mirrored halves for maximum graphic punch
-   📐 **ruleofthirds** — subject on a third line for a relaxed, editorial balance

### Behind-the-Scenes Commands For Authenticity

BTS-style images perform well because they feel unstaged, even when fully generated. These commands expose the “production” around the shot.

-   🎬 **BTS** — the polished shot steps back to reveal the crew and gear
-   📹 **camerarig** — camera body, lens, and monitor become visible props
-   🔀 **beforeafter** — raw setup and finished frame side by side

### Camera Angle Commands For Instant Drama

The camera position alone can make a subject feel powerful, vulnerable, or immersive without changing anything else in the scene.

-   🦅 **birdsview** — straight-down framing that flattens the scene into shapes
-   👤 **POV** — first-person framing that drops the viewer into the moment
-   📉 **lowangle** — shooting from below makes the subject feel towering
-   🎥 **dutchangle** — a tilted horizon injects tension and energy

### FAQ

**Do these commands work in every ChatGPT plan?**

_Yes, the commands are prompt language, not a special feature, so they work anywhere ChatGPT can generate images, including the free tier with image limits._

**Can I combine commands from different categories?**

_Yes, and it’s often better. A lighting command plus a framing command plus a style command usually outperforms using three commands from the same category._

**Why does ChatGPT sometimes ignore a command?**

_Overloaded prompts confuse the model. Keep it to three or four commands per prompt, and place the subject description first so the model has a clear anchor._

**Are these commands the same as Midjourney parameters?**

_No, Midjourney uses technical flags like `--ar` or `--stylize`. These are descriptive keywords that work because ChatGPT’s image model responds to natural language, not command-line syntax._

**Do I need to type the slash symbol?**

_No, the slash is a mnemonic from the original format, not a functional trigger. What actually works is the descriptive phrase, so typing “golden hour lighting” works the same as typing “/goldenhour.”_

### The Real Advantage Is Repetition, Not Memorization

Nobody needs to memorize 100 commands. What works is picking five or six across lighting, framing, and style that fit your niche and using them consistently until they become second nature.
