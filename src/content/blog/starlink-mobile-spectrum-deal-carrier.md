SpaceX is moving to become a US wireless carrier. On October 8, the company said it will buy a nationwide block of **800 MHz low-band spectrum** for Starlink Mobile, a deal that still needs FCC approval. A day earlier, the FCC approved its plan for 15,000 Starlink Mobile satellites.

Nothing changes for your phone bill today. SpaceX has not announced prices, a launch date or supported phones. But the deal targets the biggest weakness of satellite service: getting a signal indoors.

## What did SpaceX announce?

According to [Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/08/spacex-moves-to-acquire-nationwide-low-band-spectrum-for-starlink-mobile/), SpaceX and the investment firm Grain Management said SpaceX will acquire 14 MHz of paired spectrum in the 800 MHz band. Financial terms were not disclosed.

Grain bought that spectrum from T-Mobile in a deal announced in early 2025 and closed in August for $2.9 billion in cash plus additional licenses, per the same report. T-Mobile is not a party to the SpaceX deal.

Low-band signals travel farther and pass through walls better than the 2 GHz mid-band spectrum Starlink Mobile already holds. SpaceX says the new band will act as a "coverage layer" that reaches indoors, and that most phones already support it, according to [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/09/spacex-to-become-major-mobile-carrier-in-us-with-starlink-mobile-upgrades/).

## What did the FCC approve?

The FCC approved SpaceX's Starlink Mobile Gen2 application, which covers 15,000 satellites optimized for 2 GHz spectrum. SpaceX says the new satellites deliver more than 100 times the bandwidth of the current generation.

The FCC also granted a waiver that lets SpaceX offer wireless service through its satellites without a spectrum lease from a terrestrial carrier, [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/09/spacex-starlink-mobile-carrier/) reports. That waiver matters because it removes the need for a partnership with AT&T, Verizon or T-Mobile.

## Why are carriers worried?

SpaceX says it wants to be "the first network operator to deploy both satellite and terrestrial spectrum." Per MacRumors, shares of AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile fell after the announcement, and Verizon had its worst one-day drop since 2002.

The picture is more complicated than the stock move. SpaceX already powers T-Mobile's satellite texting feature, and MacRumors notes T-Mobile has since removed SpaceX branding from its website after finalizing a satellite venture with AT&T and Verizon.

## What does it mean for you?

For now, very little. The 800 MHz purchase is still pending, and SpaceX has not said when Starlink Mobile service will start, what it will cost, or which phones will work. Your current carrier is not affected.

If you live in a rural area or travel through cellular dead zones, this is the story to follow. A satellite network that also works indoors would be the first real alternative to a traditional plan in those places.
