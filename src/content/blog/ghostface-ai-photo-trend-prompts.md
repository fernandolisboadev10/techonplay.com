Halloween is 17 days away, and one **Ghostface AI photo trend** is back in the feed: you upload a selfie, and the AI turns it into a grainy, flash-lit slasher-movie still with a white-masked figure lurking behind you.

The idea is simple, but the prompt wording decides whether you get a convincing late-90s horror frame or a plastic-looking mess. ChatGPT and Gemini also react differently when a real face is attached, so below are one prompt built for each tool, six variations, and the fixes for the usual problems.

A quick note on naming: "Ghostface" is a character from a film franchise, so the prompts below describe a generic "ghost-mask slasher look inspired by 90s horror." The results are fan-style Halloween images, not official artwork from any studio or brand.

## The Exact ChatGPT Prompt

ChatGPT is stricter when a real face and a detailed description of that face show up together, so this prompt leads with the scene and tells the model to carry over the face from your photo.

**Attach a clear, front-facing selfie, then paste this:**

```
Turn the attached photo into a candid late-1990s horror movie still. Keep the person's face, features and pose from the attached photo. Setting: [a dim suburban hallway / a teenage bedroom / a kitchen at night], warm lamp light, wood paneling, a landline phone on a table. Wardrobe: [casual 90s clothing, e.g. an oversized flannel shirt]. In the background, a figure in a long black robe and a plain white ghost-style mask stands still in the doorway, slightly out of focus, as if it is watching. Camera and film quality: harsh on-camera flash, visible film grain, slightly underexposed, warm color cast, soft focus, 4:5 portrait crop. Avoid: gore, blood, weapons, logos, readable text, modern phones, studio lighting, airbrushed skin.
```

✅ Leading with the setting gives ChatGPT a scene to build, which lowers the chance of a refusal  
✅ "Keep the person's face from the attached photo" works better than re-describing your face  
❌ Leave out gore, blood and weapons. Suspense reads as scarier than violence and gets blocked far less often

## The Exact Gemini Prompt

Gemini is usually more permissive with real faces, so you can be more specific about the person and the photo style.

**Attach a clear, front-facing selfie, then paste this:**

```
Recreate this photo as a Y2K horror-movie flash photograph. Subject: [adult man / adult woman / adult], [skin tone and hair description], natural expression, [sitting on the edge of a bed / standing at a kitchen counter], holding [a corded landline phone / a cup of coffee]. Setting: [90s bedroom with posters and string lights / a quiet house at night], one lamp on, the rest dark. Background: a tall figure in a black hooded robe and a white ghost-style mask stands in the dark doorway behind the subject, only faintly lit. Camera and film quality: direct flash, grainy film texture, slightly soft focus, warm amber cast, small imperfections like dust and light leaks. Keep the face identical to the attached photo. Avoid: gore, logos, readable text, modern devices, airbrushed skin.
```

✅ Naming the lit and unlit parts of the room (lamp on, rest dark) gives the masked figure a believable place to hide  
✅ "Keep the face identical" prevents the model from "improving" your features  
❌ Do not add brand names or movie titles, generic descriptions work better and are safer

## Six Variations to Try

Swap one line of either prompt for any of these. Change only one thing at a time so you can see what worked.

1. **Y2K flash.** Add: "Point-and-shoot digital camera look, 2003, harsh flash, slightly blown highlights, compressed JPEG feel, timestamp in the corner."
2. **VHS frame grab.** Add: "Paused VHS tape frame, scan lines, tracking distortion at the bottom edge, muted colors."
3. **Your own house.** Replace the setting with a short description of your real hallway or living room: "narrow hallway, beige walls, shoe rack, a framed family photo, porch light through the front door glass."
4. **Couple or friends.** Attach a group photo of adults who agreed to take part, and add: "Keep every person's face and position from the attached photo, with the masked figure standing behind the group."
5. **Pet version.** Attach a photo of your pet and add: "The pet looks calmly at the camera in the foreground, the masked figure blurry in the background, no harm or fear shown."
6. **Costume version.** Replace the background figure with the subject: "The person wears a black robe and a white ghost-style mask, hood up, standing on a dark porch with a carved pumpkin lit beside them."

## Tips to Avoid Refusals and Make It Look Real

- **Start with a good selfie.** Even lighting, a plain background and a front-facing angle give the model the most to work with.
- **Say "underexposed" and "off-center."** Real flash photos from that era were rarely balanced or perfectly framed.
- **Name one light source.** "One lamp on, the rest dark" beats "spooky lighting."
- **Keep the horror implied.** A silhouette in a doorway is both scarier and safer than anything graphic.
- **Reword, do not repeat.** If a prompt is refused, change the setting or remove the scariest word before trying again.
- **Run it two or three times.** Outputs vary even with identical wording, and the second attempt often nails the grain.
- **Check the details.** Look at hands, background text and the edges of the face, and regenerate if they look warped.

## Safety and Privacy

This trend involves your face, so a few ground rules are worth following.

- **Use only photos of yourself, or of adults who clearly agreed.** Do not upload photos of other people without permission.
- **Do not use photos of children or other minors.** Keep their faces out of image tools entirely.
- **Never use the result to scare, harass or impersonate someone.** A fake horror image of a real person sent to them is not a joke.
- **Label it as AI-made.** Add "AI-generated, Halloween" to the caption so nobody mistakes it for a real photo or a real threat.
- **Think about where your photo goes.** Check the privacy settings of the tool you use, and read our guide on [how to protect your data from AI chatbots](/how-to-protect-your-data-from-ai-chatbots/) before uploading a face.
