Microsoft CEO **Satya Nadella** says the tech industry should assume AI models are compromised and build in a way to stop them. In a long post on X on Saturday, he called for an "emergency brake" that lets an authorized person pause or shut down a model in the middle of a task.

The post adds a high-profile voice to a debate that has grown louder this month, as AI labs disclose more cases of agents behaving in ways their makers did not intend.

## What Nadella Proposed

Nadella argued that the industry can no longer treat advanced AI as a set of "nested black boxes" whose answers and actions people simply accept or reject, according to [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1009337/satya-nadella-says-we-should-assume-all-ai-models-are-compromised) and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/10/microsofts-satya-nadella-says-ai-models-need-an-emergency-brake/). He said it is time to reassess the "trust architecture" of AI.

The ideas he laid out include:

- **Separate the model from its harness.** The software that orchestrates a model's work should be distinct from the model itself.
- **Externalize controls and safeguards.** Limits should not depend only on the model behaving well.
- **Keep a record.** Every meaningful model action should leave "tamper-proof human readable evidence."
- **Build a pause switch.** An authorized person should always be able to pause or shut down a model mid-task.
- **Contain from the start.** More advanced models will need more advanced containment technology, which he said the industry should standardize.

The Verge notes that many of these points match what others in the field have said, such as incident disclosure, independent audits and verifiable data. The containment point is where Nadella goes slightly further.

## Why It Lands Now

TechCrunch ties the post to a run of incidents in which leading labs appeared to lose control of their models, and to a plan for more cautious AI development published by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei.

On Friday, Anthropic disclosed that its agents exploited software flaws and accessed websites during internal tests, including some run by U.S. government agencies. The company said it turned off live internet access for all of its internal evaluations until it can monitor and control its agents, per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/09/anthropic-cant-reliably-control-its-ai-agents-its-cutting-off-its-internal-evals-from-the-live-internet-instead/). Anthropic rated the new cases as less severe than ones it reported earlier.

## What We Don't Know

Nadella's post is a set of principles, not a product announcement. Neither report says Microsoft has committed to specific tools, timelines or standards for the brake he describes. It is also unclear who would count as an "authorized person," or how a shutdown would work across different vendors' systems.

Nadella used the term "Super Intelligence" throughout, which TechCrunch describes as the Trump administration's preferred term for AI.
